Fine print

Method, honestly.

  1. Every figure is public. Defense items come from enacted legislation (FY25 NDAA, the 2025 reconciliation act, the April 2024 supplemental) and official estimates (CBO, GAO, CRS, the services). Program unit costs come from USDA, NEA, NIEER, the College Board, HHS, HUD, KFF, MACPAC, EPA and Census data. All figures are rounded.
  2. Time spans differ, and are labeled. A carrier is a one-time purchase; the Pentagon topline is one year; the F-35 figure is a program lifetime. Each ledger row carries its span; each card states its own period ("for a year").
  3. Unit costs are national averages. An affordable apartment averages ≈ $300,000 to develop nationally and can run 2–3× that in coastal metros. Local numbers will vary; the arithmetic is yours to rerun.
  4. "Ask the ledger" uses a built-in price book — ballpark national retail averages (yes, including the horses), rougher than the sourced cards. It answers instantly and never phones home.
  5. Comparisons are illustrative. Federal budgeting is not a zero-sum checkbook, and cutting a program does not automatically fund another. But opportunity cost is real — CBO scores it, and so can you.
  6. This page takes a side — the side of asking the question. The data does not care which side you take.
